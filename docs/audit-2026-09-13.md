# Audit — 2026-09-13

The dealer asked, with the mahogany line complete: audit the app and the code,
optimize what can be optimized, make sure there is no ghost code, make it as
smooth as possible, put pictures on the estimate and the order sheet, and say
what would make the app feel modern and fresh. This is what was found and what
was done. No price, number or id changed.

## Ghost code

A scan of every function, constant, element id and CSS class in both apps and
the engine, with comments stripped before counting references:

| | Defined | Never referenced |
|---|---|---|
| `quoter/index.html` | 518 functions and constants | 0 |
| `index.html` (Price Manager) | 257 | 0 |
| `packages/pricing-engine/index.js` | 124 | 0 |

Three element ids in the quoter were carried by nothing (`tierBuilder`,
`tierRetail`, `tierWrap`; the buttons are wired by class and `data-tier`) and
are gone.
`tabRaw` in the Price Manager stays: its siblings are addressed by id and it
reads as one of a set. Two Price Manager classes the scan flagged (`g-pre`,
`g-unf`) are built at run time from a column group and are live.

The one real ghost was the **no-Tailwind fallback stylesheet**: a hundred
lines of `.no-tw` rules, an `onerror` bootstrap and four comments, all there
for the day `cdn.tailwindcss.com` did not answer. It is gone, because the
reason for it is gone (next section). The suites that asserted the fallback
now assert the real styling.

Data keys no app reads — `schemaVersion`, `generatedAt`, `priceUnit` on the
catalog; `salesTaxNote`, `depositBasis`, `crateNote`, `brandColor` on the
estimate file — are documentation for whoever edits those files, and stay.

## Optimized

- **No third party at run time.** The quoter compiled its stylesheet in the
  browser on every load from the Tailwind play CDN (a 300 KB script, a flash
  of unstyled markup, a hard dependency on one host) and fetched two typefaces
  from Google. Now `tools/build-css.sh` compiles the stylesheet ahead of time
  into `quoter/assets/tw.css` (20 KB; theme in `tools/tailwind.config.cjs`)
  and the two faces ship in `quoter/assets/fonts/` as latin variable fonts
  (86 KB together, preloaded). The page paints styled on its first frame and
  works on a job site with no network. CI rebuilds the sheet and fails if the
  committed one is stale.
- **Cards below the fold cost nothing** until they scroll near
  (`content-visibility: auto`), which matters on the fiberglass line's long
  grid.
- **Every browser suite now runs against the real styling**, not the
  fallback: what the suites see is what a customer sees, and no suite touches
  the network.

Already sound and left alone: models are grouped once per scope and cached
across keystrokes; the search box is debounced; cards render in chunks of 48
behind an intersection observer; every price comes from the engine, never the
page.

## Smooth

- A photograph fades up over its frame once it has decoded rather than
  popping in; the frame holds a warm ground tone until then.
- The detail sheet settles into place with a short motion; the drawer already
  slid. Both, and the fade, honour `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Keyboard focus is visible everywhere (a brass ring), pointer focus stays
  quiet.
- The first screen's two cards show a real door of each line, not a drawn
  silhouette.

## Pictures on the printed pages

Both documents now carry a picture beside every line: the customer's estimate
so the customer sees the door they are signing for, the order sheet so
Hoelscher's desk sees the door beside the part number. The picture is the one
the quote line means — the grille design chosen (a true picture of that door
with that grille), else the door's own catalog photograph, else a picture of
the same door from another catalog page, else the photograph of the door it is
a variation of. A sidelite in the opening gets its own small picture beside
its line. The quote drawer's thumbnails follow the same rule, so the screen
and the paper agree. Printing waits until every picture has decoded, so none
prints as a blank box. `tests/print.test.mjs` pins all of it.

## Modern and fresh — done, and what is left to decide

Done in this pass: the compiled stylesheet and shipped fonts (the page no
longer flashes), the photograph fade, the sheet motion, the focus rings, the
real doors on the first screen, and the whitened glass from the day before.

Worth doing next, each a small change, none started because each is a
judgment the dealer should make:

1. **Card hover preview.** Hovering a card could show the door's second
   picture (the decorative or grille variant) or its sizes and price range,
   so a sales person reads more without opening the sheet.
2. **Sticky "Add to quote" on the phone.** The price and the button sit at
   the bottom of a long sheet; pinning them while the questions scroll would
   save a swipe per door.
3. **A quote summary strip** at the top of the catalogue once a quote has
   lines — count, total, "print" — so the drawer is not the only place the
   running total lives on a desktop.
4. **Recently viewed / compare.** Two or three doors side by side with their
   prices, for a customer choosing.
5. **Dark mode.** The palette (ink, paper, brass, forest) inverts cleanly and
   the printed pages are already their own black-on-white stylesheet; a
   `prefers-color-scheme` variant is a day's work.
6. **A short welcome for the customer mode**: one line under the header
   saying prices are retail and a sales person will confirm, since customers
   now share the screen.

## Verification

Seventeen suites, 633 checks, all green; the quoter suite runs on the compiled
stylesheet and asserts the shipped fonts loaded, the print suite asserts the
pictures on both documents and that print waited for them.
